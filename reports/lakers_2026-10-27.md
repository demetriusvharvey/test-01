# Lakers–Portland loss review: October 27, 2026

## What the record actually shows

The legacy model recorded Lakers moneyline before the game at 9:48 p.m. ET, with a 0.56907 model probability. Its best recorded quote was -125 (DraftKings/BetMGM/etc.); the FanDuel snapshot was -126 at 9:00 p.m. ET. Portland won 136–129. This is one simulated recommendation, not evidence of an actual wager. Sources: the frozen `data/predictions.json` ledger and [AP game recap](https://www.cbssports.com/nba/gametracker/recap/NBA_20261027_POR%40LAL/).

At -125 the break-even rate is 55.56%; at -126 it is 55.75%. A single 56.907% prediction losing is compatible with ordinary uncertainty. The game contributes one legacy-model Brier observation of approximately 0.324; one observation cannot measure calibration or prove a defect. The old forecast was a market/Elo blend, not the new pure-model forecast.

[The Odds Gap](https://theoddsgap.com/odds/nba/portland-trail-blazers-vs-los-angeles-lakers/2026-10-27) reports FanDuel -120 at 11:12 p.m. ET as its last pre-start quote, and lists a game time of 11:13 p.m.; the public schedule listed 11:00 p.m. The actual tip and exact closing time were not independently verified. Treat the reported quote as a potentially useful price observation, not a verified sharp close or a quantified CLV result.

## What changes, and what does not

The loss exposed the need to preserve source timing, differentiate a FanDuel quote from the best price at another book, avoid assuming the user wagered, track disagreement with the market, and verify injuries and lineups before trusting a projection. Ledger v2 records those distinctions for new paper selections and preserves the old record. The existing uncalibrated model and incomplete injury data remain clearly marked. No weight, referee factor, or betting rule is tuned to this one result; we do not claim to know the cause of the loss from one game.

Next, collect genuinely pregame injury and lineup observations and independent sharp prices, then evaluate challengers chronologically across many games and seasons. Missing time-of-knowledge records remain missing. A repeated assessment against a matched-time market and a truly untouched holdout is required before any feature can be promoted.
